# 11. Altruistic Punishment: Taking ¥0 Rather Than Letting You Take ¥900

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **The market has no fairness, morality or malice — only prices.** The moment you anthropomorphize the market, "punishing it" becomes your decision basis — the only decision basis guaranteed to lose both ways.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The scene: the ultimatum game.** Player A is given ¥1000 and proposes a split to a stranger, B. If B accepts, both are paid as proposed; if B refuses, **both get ¥0**.
  - A offers ¥100, keeps ¥900.
  - Classical economics says B should accept — "¥100 beats ¥0."
  - **Reality: B refuses in anger** — taking nothing rather than letting A take ¥900.
- **Two mechanisms:**
  - ⚖️ **Inequity aversion** — people dislike unfair outcomes, with an asymmetry: **being on the disadvantaged side of unfairness stings far more than being on the advantaged side.**
  - 🛑 **Altruistic punishment** — the willingness to pay a personal cost to punish a fairness-violator. Note the word "altruistic": the punisher bears the cost; the group collects the benefit.
- **The neural layer:** feeling unfairly treated activates the **insula** — the disgust center — and people accept real economic loss to discharge it. **Punishment doesn't produce a gain; it terminates a disgust state.**
- **The most underrated generalization:** the fairness instinct is not an "extra preference" — it is **a priceable force**. Any system assuming participants maximize only in the money dimension will meet "irrational" behavior it can't price when fairness is violated. **It isn't noise; it's a second objective function.**

> *"Inequity aversion: people dislike unfair outcomes — and hate being on the disadvantaged side of unfairness far more than the advantaged side."* (paraphrased; confidence: medium)

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** I filed fairness under "civilizational lubricant" — nice, but a moral matter, irrelevant to trading decisions. I considered myself purely calculating in markets.
- **After:** I answered the terminology question correctly (inequity aversion + altruistic punishment) — but only half of it, because **I hadn't yet noticed the mechanism firing in me**. The turn came in the deep-dive on social preferences, which listed three live market forms of the same mechanism:
  - **Revenge trading** — after a gap-down blows through the stop, the trader anthropomorphizes "the market" into an enemy that stole his wealth, and feels the urge to strike back.
  - **Short-squeeze martyrdom** — in a violent rally, shorts refuse to cover on a **fairness verdict** ("this company is worth nothing"), add out of spite, and get liquidated by the squeeze.
  - **Retail revenge adds** — shaken out of a position, the "irrational win-back" or the spite reversal — the market-maker's favorite liquidity ATM.
  **All three are the same mechanism:** aiming moral demands that only apply to *persons* at a system that has no intentions — and paying real money for it.
- **The third layer:** this produced rules 23 and 24. **Rule 23 — erase anthropomorphic moral judgments:** the market has no "fairness," "morality," or "manipulators"; price is the objective result of marginal matching; any resistance position justified by "the whales are rigging it" or "fundamentals don't deserve this price" is banned. **Rule 24 — mandatory 48-hour cooldown after a stop-out:** the moment a stop fires, the instrument goes onto a 48-hour no-entry blacklist; same-day reversal or re-entry is forbidden. **The second rule is the enforcement of the first**: erasing moral judgment by cognition alone is not enough; it needs a physical barrier.

```text
Anti-revenge-trading protocol (an emotional settlement system)

  Rule 23 · Language audit
    Banned in decision records: "whales" "rigging" "unfair"
    "doesn't deserve this price" "how dare it"
    → any occurrence: tag anthropomorphizing, pause the decision
    → replace with: "a cause I don't know" / "information not yet priced"

  Rule 24 · 48-hour no-entry
    Stop-out on an instrument → automatic 48-hour blacklist
    → no same-day reversal or re-entry on the same instrument
    → reason: physically block the revenge-trading impulse

  Universal · Lose-lose check
    Before any decision carrying a "punish" flavor (refusing a deal,
    spite reversal, cancelling out of anger), fill in:
    my loss = ?    their loss = ?
    → if "my loss > 0 AND their behavior won't change" → don't execute
```
