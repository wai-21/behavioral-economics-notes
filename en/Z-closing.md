# 🧩 Closing: What This Archive Actually Changed

Nine chapters and 24 rules later, if only three sentences may remain:

**One — bias is an environment problem, not an intelligence problem.**
My original default fix was "try harder": compare harder, self-discipline harder, stay objective harder. Across nine chapters, that fix won exactly zero times. What worked was changing friction: the phone moved to the living room, the stop-loss written as a mechanical order, the good option set as the default. **Changing the environment is cheaper than changing yourself.**

**Two — wrong answers are worth more than right ones.**
The parts of this archive I'm least willing to delete are not the nine correct quiz answers — they're the three faceplants: judging "123456" as outside availability, accepting a spoiled answer fed by a leaked label, and the "1" that got read as a math question. Correct answers only prove I memorized the vocabulary. **Wrong answers expose where my actual understanding ends.**

**Three — the audit must happen outside of "me."**
The last layer, and the most counterintuitive: the thing this system can least rely on is me — System 2 is a consumable, biases defend themselves, the me in the loss zone is not the me in the gain zone, and wrong answers arrive first with full confidence. So the real way out is moving the checkpoints out of human hands: into rules, into defaults, into a process that never fatigues.

**That is why this archive ends not with "I understood behavioural economics," but with "I left myself 24 gates — pressed by someone other than me."**
