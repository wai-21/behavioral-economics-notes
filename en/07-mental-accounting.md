# 07. Mental Accounting: Why Lottery Money Doesn't Feel Like Your Own

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Every pool of money must use the same risk-pricing formula.** Assets are perfectly fungible — loosening the management of a side account erodes the long-run compound geometric return; it is not "diluted by that one small amount."

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The scene: two identical ¥3000.**
  - A scratch-card win of ¥3000 → an unhesitating feast with friends that night, spent in full.
  - The next week, ¥3000 of hard overtime pay → carefully banked, untouchable.
  - Identical purchasing power.
- **The mechanism: mental accounting (Thaler).** We file money into virtual accounts by **source, storage, or purpose**, and treat the accounts with radically different attitudes:
  - 💼 **Sweat-money account**: salary, overtime — spent grudgingly, to the cent.
  - 🎁 **Windfall account**: lottery, red packets, tax refunds — "free money," spent painlessly.
  - 📦 **Shipping account**: filed as "pure loss / wasted money" — every extra yuan stings.
  - 🛍️ **Goods account**: filed as "consumption with consideration" — an extra ¥100 trinket feels like "at least I got something."
- **Two forms worth memorizing:**
  - **The free-shipping threshold:** item ¥420 + ¥80 shipping = ¥500; or add a useless ¥100 trinket = ¥520. **Spending more feels cheaper** — because ¥80 of shipping lands in the "wasted money" account while the ¥100 trinket lands in the "real goods" account.
  - **Points vs cash discount:** the same ¥1000 benefit. An instant ¥1000 discount lowers payment pain; "1000 points, valid one month" lands in the windfall account — and the expiry creates "spend it or waste it" pressure that **induces a second purchase**.
- **The theoretical kill shot: fungibility fails.** Classical economics is built on "a yuan is a yuan." Mental accounting shows that foundation fails **psychologically**.

> *"Classical economics holds that money is fungible — but mental accounting shows we divide money into separate virtual accounts by source, storage or purpose."* (paraphrased; attribution to Thaler — confidence: high)

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** I read mental accounting as "consumer psychology" — a merchant's trick to defend against. My posture was purely defensive: spot the trap, don't fall in.
- **After:** three questions flipped my role from "the trapped" to "the one opening accounts on myself." On the scratch-card question I answered correctly. On the free-shipping question my answer — "mental accounting" — was directionally right. On the points question I said **"the points are free money"** — the dialogue confirmed: *"You nailed the key."* **The real gain was my own wording:** "free money" is four characters that *perform the account-opening act itself*. I wasn't describing the bias — I was **opening a new account on the spot**.
- **The third layer:** if I open the accounts, I can **close them**. So de-biasing isn't a one-time cognitive task but a standing rule: **unrealized gains, windfalls and small amounts all get the same weight as core capital.** The mindset "I'm playing with profits" is itself an account opening — it moves the risk into a fresh account that owes no accountability.

```text
Account-merging rules (personal capital layer)

  ① Naming check
     When I say "it's free money / it's earned back / it's just profits /
     it's only a little" → tag an account opening; pause the decision;
     restate the amount in core-capital terms

  ② Equal-weight pricing
     Every source of funds uses the exact same position caps, stop rules
     and expected-value thresholds as core capital
     → nothing is relaxed because "the money came easy"

  ③ Shipping and fees, reclassified
     Shipping, handling, subscriptions are not "wasted money" — they are
     acquisition costs
     → compare TOTAL outlay, not the price of the visible good
     → buying ¥100 of unneeded stuff to dodge ¥80 of shipping is paying
       ¥100 for psychological comfort

  ④ Limited-time offer signal
     "Limited time / expires / only X days left" → tag as account opening
     + hot state, double signal → no decision on the spot
```
