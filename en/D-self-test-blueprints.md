# 🧪 Appendix D: Self-Test Pack & Two Blueprints

> **This appendix is a tool, not a read.** Part one: the 16 multiple-choice + 2 open questions reconstructed to be answerable standalone, answers folded at the end. Part two: two blueprints you can copy as-is. **Every question carries my original answer — the wrong one is where the value is.**

## Part 1 · Self-test (answer first, then check)

**Group A · Chapter quizzes (7 questions)**

**A1** (topic 03) A friend privately vouches that empty Restaurant A is a hidden gem. On site, A is empty and B has a long queue. In behavioural economics, "ignoring your own private information and following the crowd" most directly causes:
A. All restaurants automatically cut prices 　**B. Valuable private information gets buried, and the group collectively makes second-best decisions** 　C. Consumers compute more efficiently 　D. Market information asymmetry disappears

**A2** (topic 04) Which phenomenon **least** reflects the availability heuristic?
A. Avoiding flights for months after crash news 　B. Using "123456" as a password 　**C. Collecting five years of claims data and calmly computing the optimal plan** 　D. Habitually renewing with the old telecom

**A3** (topic 06) Which behavior is mainly driven by loss aversion?
**A. Refusing to realize a falling stock's loss and averaging down hoping to break even** 　B. Buying two drinks on the "second at 40% off" deal 　C. Buying after a shop assistant's warm hospitality 　D. Buying a rolling alarm clock

**A4** (topic 08) Which is a precommitment strategy?
**A. An automatic payday transfer into a restricted deposit account** 　B. Finishing expensive ice cream at a buffet because "not eating is a loss" 　C. Buying the new smartwatch because a friend did 　D. Buying sale clothes on a random stroll

**A5** (topic 10) Which scenario best shows System 1 dominating in a hot state?
**A. Starving in a supermarket: went in for water, left with a cart of snacks** 　B. Comparing three banks' mortgage rates 　C. Two fixed study hours daily for a month 　D. A pre-retirement financial planning session

**A6** (topic 12) Per Keynes's beauty contest, what do speculators care about most?
A. Thirty years of intrinsic value 　**B. "What price the crowd will shortly believe others will pay"** 　C. IFRS compliance 　D. Personal product preference

**A7** (topic 13) Which design best fits the nudge philosophy?
A. Legal mandate: drive two days less per week, heavy fines 　B. Trap clauses in tiny font 　**C. Auto-enrolled pension contributions with free opt-out at any time** 　D. Banning all sugary drinks

**Group B · Cross-chapter (5 questions)**

**B1** (status quo + loss aversion) Offered a 5% raise for near-identical work, Xiao Ming stays: "adapting is risky, and I'm used to the team." Which combo? **B. Status-quo bias + loss aversion**

**B2** (mental accounting) ¥3000 lottery win spent the same night; ¥3000 overtime pay carefully banked. The concept? **B. Mental accounting**

**B3** (social preference) In the ultimatum game, B refuses ¥100 out of ¥1000, taking ¥0 rather than letting A keep ¥900. Which mechanism? **B. Inequity aversion + altruistic punishment**

**B4** (CRT) Bat and ball cost $1.10; the bat costs $1.00 more. Intuition's wrong answer and System 2's true answer? **B. Intuition $0.10 / truth $0.05**

**B5** (present bias + nudge) Kenya's harvest-time, small, time-limited fertilizer vouchers — which bias do they exploit and overcome? **C. Present bias and procrastination**

**Group C · Anti-marketing (4 questions)**

**C1** (endowment + status quo) "Free for 30 days, then auto-renews at ¥300" — which two biases? **B. Endowment effect + status-quo bias**

**C2** (decoy) Cinema popcorn Small 100 / Large 190, then Medium 180 added; Small buyers switch to Large. The Medium's purpose? **B. A decoy making the Large look like a ¥10 upgrade**

**C3** (framing / Rule of 100) Why do merchants quote percentage discounts for cheap items and absolute amounts for expensive ones? **A. The brain is impressed by the absolute size of numbers — 6000 shocks more than 20**

**C4** (anchoring + accounts + hot state) Facing "add one item at ¥9.9" on the payment page, which self-talk best re-engages System 2? **B. "If this were listed alone on the homepage at ¥9.9, would I click in and buy it?"**

## Part 2 · Blueprint 1: Anti-Marketing-Trick Table

> **Usage:** treat the left column as recognition signals, the right column as conditioned responses. **Recognition doesn't take intelligence — only exposure.**

| Merchant move | Biases hit | Why it works on the spot | **Counter-move (memorize)** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Free-shipping threshold** (free over ¥500; else ¥80) | Mental accounting + loss aversion | Shipping lands in the "wasted money" account; a ¥100 unneeded trinket lands in the "real goods" account | Compare total outlay, not item price. **Pick whichever of "¥80 shipping + ¥420 item" vs "¥520 item" costs less** — never pay ¥100 for comfort |
| **Points vs cash discount** (pay ¥10000, get 1000 points valid one month) | Mental accounting (windfall) + scarcity/urgency | Points are filed as "free money"; the deadline adds "spend it or waste it" pressure | Convert points 1:1 to cash before comparing; **"limited time" is itself a hot-state signal → no decision on the spot** |
| **Free trial + auto-renew** (free month, then ¥300/month) | Endowment effect + status-quo bias | Playlists and history create ownership; auto-renew is the default | **Write the cancellation date into the calendar at signup** (not "remember to cancel"); treat the trial as a subscription sales package |
| **Decoy option** (S 100 / M 180 / L 190) | Decoy effect (asymmetric dominance) | Almost nobody buys the Medium; it moves the battlefield from "100 vs 190" to "180 vs 190" | Ask first: **"which option here would almost nobody buy?"** → that's the decoy → remove it and re-compare the rest |
| **Framing · Rule of 100** (percentages on cheap, absolutes on pricey) | Framing effect + number intuition | The fast-scanning brain is impressed by absolute magnitude: 6000 shocks more than 20 | Normalize: **convert everything to the same measure** (amount paid or percent), then compare |
| **Checkout-page "add one item"** (¥30 struck through; add-on ¥9.9) | Anchoring + mental accounting + hot state (countdown) | Three layers stack: 9.9 feels dirt cheap → "the order is already ¥300" → rushing to finish | **Context-strip question:** "If it were on the homepage alone at ¥9.9, would I click in for it?" |

## Part 3 · Blueprint 2: The Bedtime Nudge Loop

> **Background:** this is not advice — it's the blueprint the source forced out of me step by step, with every trade-off recorded. Copy it as-is.

```text
Problem framing
  └─ Staying up late = classic time inconsistency
       Daytime (cold): know early sleep pays off tomorrow
       Midnight (hot): willpower depleted, System 1 dominant, "one more clip"

Trigger identification
  └─ Main activity: short-video scrolling
       The platform drives friction to ~zero: autoplay (default/status quo)
       + high-frequency micro-rewards (a dopamine hit every ~15 seconds)

Strategy choice (three options; I took the lowest-friction one)
  ① Physical isolation (raise operating cost)   ← chosen
  ② Digital lock (precommitment)
  ③ Visual downgrade (reduce temptation appeal)

├─ Step 1 · Kill the exception excuse (critical; most-skipped)
│    My stated obstacle: "I need the phone as my alarm"
│    → unresolved, the isolation gets legally revoked by myself
│
├─ Step 2 · Choose the plan (trade-off between A/B)
│    A. Buy a separate alarm clock; the phone loses its bedroom excuse
│    B. Keep the phone as the alarm — farthest room, max volume    ← chosen
│    Reason: no new purchase; physical distance raises the first-move cost;
│          also kills the in-bed scrolling trigger
│
├─ Step 3 · Write it as If-Then (no deliberation when tired)
│    "After brushing my teeth → phone to the dining table, charging,
│     max-volume alarm on"
│    ▸ Key mechanism: habit stacking — attached to "brushing teeth,"
│      an existing daily habit; near-zero memory load
│
└─ Step 4 · Patch the sewer (find the weakest link)
     Ask: which step breaks first?
     → answer: "the instant I walk over to silence the alarm"
        (body just awake, System 2 not booted; easy to sink onto the
         couch and start scrolling)
     Patch: "after silencing the alarm, eyes go to the window →
        curtains open, sunlight in"
     Mechanism: light suppresses melatonin, raises cortisol,
        accelerates System 2 boot-up

Final loop
  🪥 Evening trigger: after brushing teeth → phone to the dining table,
     charging + max-volume alarm
  ⏰ Morning response: walk to the table, silence the alarm →
     curtains open immediately, sunlight in
```

**Three transferable points (any habit):**

1. **The trigger must attach to an existing habit** — attaching to "I'll remember" is attaching to nothing.
2. **Actively kill the exception excuses** — otherwise the isolation gets revoked by yourself, legally ("I need the phone as my alarm" is the classic).
3. **Patch the sewer** — ask "which step breaks first"; that step needs a **no-decision-required** action to catch it.
