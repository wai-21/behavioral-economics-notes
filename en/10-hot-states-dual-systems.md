# 10. Hot States: Why You Order in Anger and Want a Refund by Morning

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **In a hot state, no irreversible actions.** The "24-hour delay" is not advice; it's a hard rule. The only action a hot state reliably supports is no action.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The scene: shopping while furious.** A terrible day at work; scrolling the phone on the commute home; the limited-edition sneakers you always called overpriced; a voice inside: "I've suffered enough today — don't I deserve a reward?" — order placed without hesitation. **The next morning, calm returns, the payment notification lands, and regret sets in.**
- **Two mechanisms:**
  - 🧠 **Dual-process theory**:
    - **System 1 (fast)**: intuitive, instant, emotion- and instinct-driven, nearly free to run.
    - **System 2 (slow)**: deliberate, rational, **consumes cognitive resources** — so it fatigues and can be occupied.
  - 🔥 **Hot-cold states (Loewenstein's empathy gap)** — under stress, anger, hunger or strong desire, System 1 takes over, making us myopic and prone to **impulsive, destructive** decisions.
- **Corollary 1: emotion doesn't merely make decisions "less objective" — it rewrites the decision machinery.** In a hot state you aren't miscalculating; you're **running a different algorithm**.
- **Corollary 2 (the most practical): System 2 is a consumable.** Because it burns resources, its availability declines through the day — **the decision you make at night is made by a different person than the morning one.**
- **Corollary 3 (the CRT bat-and-ball):** "A bat and a ball cost $1.10 together. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much is the ball?"
  - System 1 blurts **$0.10** (check: bat $1.10 + ball $0.10 = $1.20 — contradiction).
  - System 2 computes: x + (x + 1.00) = 1.10 → **$0.05** (bat $1.05).
  - The value of this item is that it's **replicable**: System 1's wrong answer **forces its way into consciousness with equal confidence** — "it just popped up" and "I thought it through" are subjectively indistinguishable.

> *"In hot states of stress, anger, hunger or desire, System 1 dominates, making us short-sighted and prone to impulsive, destructive decisions."* (paraphrased; confidence: medium)

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** I read emotion as "my judgment gets less accurate" — a precision loss. So the fix was "remind myself to calm down."
- **After:** two questions gave a far sharper picture. The supermarket-while-starving scenario I answered correctly — in an extreme hunger hot state, primal desire seizes control. On the CRT I also answered correctly (intuition 0.10 / truth 0.05) — **but the biggest gain wasn't the score; it was the scary fact the question exposed: System 1's wrong answer arrives first, carrying equal confidence.** It isn't "a thought" — it's **a thought disguised as a conclusion**.
- **The third layer:** "remind yourself to stay calm" fails because it mistakes System 1's output for System 2's. The correct move is **forcing a verifiable step between the two answers**. The CRT is curable because arithmetic can verify it; emotional decisions have no arithmetic — only **physical delay**. Hence the hot-state rule: the delay isn't waiting for you to calm down; it's **waiting for an environment where things can be computed**.

```text
Hot-state blocking protocol (three gates)

  Gate 1 · State check (mandatory before any irreversible decision)
    ① Physiological state right now: hungry / sleepy / angry / anxious /
      euphoric? (any hit → hot state)
    ② How long since my last meal / sleep?
    ③ Is this decision on my plan, or did it just occur to me?
    → any red light → proceed to Gate 2

  Gate 2 · Cooling (forced, no exemptions)
    □ Unplanned trade: 24-hour cooling
    □ High-price purchase: 24-hour cooling (written into the todo list;
      paying on the spot forbidden)
    □ Volatility spike / violent intraday session: no manual entries or
      adjustments of any kind

  Gate 3 · Post-hoc attribution (tag every closed decision)
    □ Did this decision come from: the standing system (System 2) /
      an intraday impulse (System 1)?
    → monthly tally of System 1 share; a rising share is the leading
      indicator of discipline decay
```
