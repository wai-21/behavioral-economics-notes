# 08. Time Inconsistency: Why the Annual Gym Card Gets Used Three Times

> **⚡ Transferable rule:** **Drive the friction of good behaviors to zero and bad behaviors to maximum.** Willpower is a consumable; friction is equipment. Consumables run out; equipment keeps working.

## 📖 What the book actually says

- **The scene: the gym membership that never gets used.**
  - 📅 **January you** (rational, far-sighted): "Three sessions a week, averages out to pennies per visit — great deal!"
  - 🛋️ **Friday-evening you** (exhausted): "The couch, the show, the chips. Next week."
  - **Result: two or three visits all year.** And — **the prepaid fee did not raise attendance**; it only made *not going* more expensive.
- **Two mechanisms:**
  - ⏳ **Time inconsistency** — with the goal in the distant future we're patient and well-planned; when the choice arrives in the present, we have no resistance to immediate temptation. Note the precise meaning: not "impatience," but **preferences flip over time** — today I promise to endure tomorrow; tomorrow I insist today deserves a treat. The classical assumption of stable time preferences fails.
  - ⚡ **Present bias** — the brain weights "immediate and visible" pleasure far above "future and abstract" benefit (fitness in six months).
- **The countermeasure: precommitment** — **deliberately restrict your future choice set to force your future self to comply.** The source's image: Odysseus lashing himself to the mast against the Sirens — **an irrevocable physical constraint substituting for an undependable willpower**.
- **The most memorable corollary: "I" am not a stable decision-making entity.** Any plan that "relies on resolve" is a bet that tomorrow-me agrees with today-me — and the mechanism guarantees they won't.

> *"To fight this weakness, people invented precommitment — deliberately restricting the future choice set to force the future self into compliance."* (paraphrased; confidence: medium)

## 💡 How it rewired my decisions

- **Before:** "I can't stick to things" was a self-discipline problem, so my toolkit was goal-setting, plans, accountability partners — **all willpower injections, all unreliable**.
- **After:** two questions cracked it open. First, the quiz: which is precommitment? — auto-deducting a fixed sum into a restricted deposit account every payday. I answered correctly: *the far-sighted self restricts the future choice set while the salary is still fresh*. Second, the Kenya fertilizer voucher (Duflo & Kremer's RCT): farmers know fertilizer pays, yet don't save after harvest; researchers hand out a **small, time-limited discount voucher at harvest time — when cash is most abundant** — and fertilizer adoption jumps. **I got that answer by spotting the AI's leaked label, not by reasoning** — it admitted the citation tag "spoiled" the answer. **The real value was the full recipe: timing (the moment of peak motivation) + small size + a deadline.** All three required. My earlier understanding of precommitment was just "lock the money"; the actual recipe is **locking behavior at the moment motivation is strongest**.
- **The third layer:** my execution template changed. From "set a goal, then rely on grit" to: **find the trigger point (an existing habit) → bind the action → raise the friction → patch the leak.** This directly produced the bedtime-nudge loop in [topic 13](13-nudge-choice-architecture.md).

```text
Precommitment design template (four elements, all mandatory)

  ① Trigger (Timing) — must attach to an existing habit
     e.g. after brushing teeth / payday / harvest day / Monday start
     Reason: zero memory cost; the behavior fires automatically (habit stacking)

  ② Lock (Lock) — physically shrink the future choice set
     e.g. auto-transfer to restricted deposit / charge the phone in the living
     room / hand the password to family
     Test: the lock must constrain CHOICES, not intentions

  ③ Window — small size + deadline
     e.g. limited-time voucher / a budget that expires in 90 days / a 1-week trial
     Reason: an unlimited commitment equals no commitment; people act
     only on expiring opportunities

  ④ Patch — seal the most fragile link in the chain
     e.g. the first action after the alarm is pre-assigned (open the curtains)
     Test: ask "which step will break first?" → that step gets a patch
```
